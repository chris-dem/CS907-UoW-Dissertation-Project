    \section*{AntipodalStrongSperner Colours}
    \label{antipodal-ss-proof}
    Proof of the claim \ref{clm:antipodal-discrete-square}
    \begin{proof}
        We are going to assume proof by contradiction. Assume there exists
        colour $j$ that is duplicated on two points $p_1 = p + b_1, p_2 = p + b_2$, such that
        $p$ is the anchor of a panchromatic solution and $b_1, b_2 \in \mathbb{B}^n$.
        Since the cube is antipodal, that means that we have to be wary of $\bar{p}_2$ which
        is at the position $p + \overline{b_2}$. Since the cube in discrete, it implies
        that $\bar{p}_2$ and $p_1$ cannot belong in any $(n-1)$-subspace, otherwise
        the cube won't be discrete. However, that would imply that $\forall i \in [n]: |[\bar{p}_2 - p_1]_i | = 1$. We know
        the only position in the hypercube that satisfies that is $\bar{p}_1$ which implies $p_1 = p_2$.
        Therefore, every corner of an antipodal discrete cube must contain a different colour.
    \end{proof}
