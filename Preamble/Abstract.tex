
\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}
\vspace{1cm}

\large
In the world of combinatorics, one should always seek the combinatorial
interpretation of numbers. Such discoveries open the world to many interesting
relations and structures which can bring forward groundbreaking results
as argued by Pak \cite{pak_WhatCombinatorialInterpretation_2022}.
This sentiment has sparked a movement where researchers have tried to answer
that question with the help of counting complexity 
\cite{pak_WhatCombinatorialInterpretation_2022,ikenmeyer_WhatWhatNot_2022}.
A particularly interesting area of choice was brought forward
by Ikenmeyer et al. \cite{ikenmeyer_WhatWhatNot_2022}, and their work on $\textbf{\#TFNP}-1$,
the combinatorial interpretation of problems whose nature guarantee at least one solution.
In our current project we focused around combinatorial interpretations 
of $\textbf{\#PPAD}-1$ class as it was revealed in \cite{ikenmeyer_WhatWhatNot_2022},
that several of its complete problems may have or may not
have a combinatorial interpertation.

In our project, we attempt to resolve the above conjecture with the help
of the \textit{PureCircuit} problem, a circuit-like problem that uses
kleene logic to find satisfying assingments.
We demonstrated how one can embed \textit{hazard-free} logic into \textbf{PPAD},
and how we can use topological problems with kleene logic to parsimoniously reduce
to the \textit{PureCircuit} problem.
We then focused on the topological interpretations of unbalanced digraphs and 
demonstated key parsimonious reduction with the \textit{EndOfLine}
and \textit{SourceOrExcess}$(2,1)$ problem.
Lastly we analysed,
deeper the effect of multi-source graphs and
their combinatorial classes which they give rise to.

Moreover with our project we are outlining an application that is able to visualise \textit{PureCircuit}
instances and validate. In addition, we were able to demonstrate the usage of metaheuristic
algorithms to find solutions. Lastly we built our own specialised backtracking algorithm
for enumerating through solutions in \textit{PureCircuit} graphs  and demonstrated substantial
improvements over the traditional brute force algorithm.
