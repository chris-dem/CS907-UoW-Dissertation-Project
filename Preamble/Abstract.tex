
\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}
\vspace{1cm}

\large
Finding combinatorial interpretations for mathematical objects—associating concrete meaning to abstract numbers—has long
been a driving force in mathematics,
with recent work by Pak and others demonstrating how counting complexity can unlock deep structural insights
\cite{pak_WhatCombinatorialInterpretation_2022,ikenmeyer_WhatWhatNot_2022}.
We built upon their idea in order to study the combinatorial structure of the $\textbf{\#PPAD}-1$ complexity class
using the \textit{PureCircuit},
a circuit-like problem that uses three-valued logic to find satisfying assignments.
Our main contribution builds upon the relation of \textit{PureCircuit} with topological structures
and its potential usages for parsimonious reductions across \textbf{PPAD} problems.

We first demonstrate how to embed three-valued logic into the \textbf{PPAD} framework and
different parsimonious reductions we can constuct across the \textit{EndOfLine} and
$\textit{SourceOrExcess}(2,1)$ and \textit{PureCircuit}.
There reductions allow us to analyse unbalanced diagraphs through a topological lens,
uncovering previously unexplored combinatorial classes within $\textbf{\#PPAD}-1$.
Moerover, our analysis extends to multi-source graph configurations, where we characterise their combinatorial structures
and demonstrate their connections between combinatorial and topological interpretations.

We complement our theoretical work with computational tools for visualising \textit{PureCircuit} instances.
We demonstrate the application of several meta-heuristic algorithms to approximate solutions
as well as a specialised backtracking algorithm for solution enumeration,
demonstrating practical improvements over standard approaches.
